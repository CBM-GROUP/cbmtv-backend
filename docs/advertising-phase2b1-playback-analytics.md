# Phase 2B.1 playback and analytics policy

## Configuration and precedence

The allocation selector remains behind `ADVERTISING_ALLOCATION_DELIVERY_ENABLED=false` by default. The existing campaign selector is preserved. Video placement settings are edited through the permission-gated Advertising Delivery workspace and audited like other placement edits.

Pre-roll, mid-roll, and post-roll placements have a skip delay (`null` means non-skippable) and a maximum accepted ad duration (1–300 seconds). A direct-client creative inherits the placement skip delay. A campaign creative uses its existing campaign `skippable_after` value instead; campaign `null` explicitly means non-skippable. This preserves historical campaign behavior. The issued skip rule is stored on the decision, so replay and event validation remain consistent if an administrator edits the placement during playback. The shared stored defaults are five seconds for direct skip, 300 seconds for duration, and two mid-roll interruptions. There is no creative or allocation playback override in this phase.

Mid-roll uses up to three ordered cue points in elapsed main-content seconds. The placement's `max_midroll_interruptions` (0–2) limits selected mid-roll ads in the viewer session, while preserving later cue opportunities if an earlier cue returned no ad. Zero disables mid-roll. The independent viewer policy still allows at most two video interruptions over the entire playback session. Resumed playback consumes earlier cues; a short title cannot trigger a cue within ten seconds of its end.

Direct allocations have independent nullable `daily_limit` and `impression_limit` fields. Defaults are 100 per UTC day and 10,000 lifetime; `null` means unlimited inventory. Campaign caps remain authoritative for campaign creatives, and the allocation still records its own impression counter. The separate viewer policy allows at most two direct exposures per advertiser per viewer session and at most two video interruptions per playback session, even with unlimited inventory. Decisions reserve cap capacity, but only qualifying impression events increment counters.

## Analytics contract

`AdRequest` records an ad request and `Decision` records the selection result; either may have no creative. An impression is a qualifying visible display or continuous visible video playback, not a selection. `video_start` is emitted when video playback starts. Quartiles require ordered predecessor events and elapsed playback at 25%, 50%, and 75% of the ready media duration. `complete` requires the ordered prior milestones and 95% duration elapsed and is emitted by the player on natural media end. `skip` requires an explicit viewer action, a started video, and both wall time and media position at or after the configured delay. A skipped play may already have an impression. `click` requires an approved destination. Abandoned or failed plays have no completion or skip unless actually recorded.

The unique `(decision, event kind)` key prevents duplicate counting; event ingestion locks the session and decision path. Event records retain the decision's client, optional campaign, creative, allocation, placement, and event time. Reporting calculates `completion_rate = complete / video_start`, `skip_rate = skip / video_start`, and `click_through_rate = click / impression` as fractions; the dashboard displays percentages. Zero denominators return `null`. Impressions are events, not unique viewers. Reports cover client, optional campaign, creative, allocation, placement, and UTC day. No demographic or revenue figures are inferred.

## Compatibility, rollout, and rollback

Migration `advertising.0010_deliveryallocation_daily_limit_and_more` adds nullable inventory caps and placement playback fields. Migration `advertising.0011_decision_playback_policy` adds an empty policy snapshot to historical decisions and stores the issued skip rule for new video decisions. Existing direct allocations receive conservative stored cap defaults and placement defaults; historical decisions, events, and campaign counters are untouched. Public response fields are unchanged; playback sessions add optional `max_midroll_interruptions` alongside their already supported cue list. Existing website clients use the cue list and `skippable_after` response value without a player API change.

Apply migrations with the allocation flag off. Check configuration and recorded events in staging before enabling allocation selection. Turning the flag off returns new opportunities to legacy campaign selection; the emergency flag and operational pause stop both strategies. Do not reverse the migration while Phase 2B.1 servers are running.

## Local performance sample and remaining risk

On disposable PostgreSQL 16 with 1,000 image allocations across 100 clients, 20 selected decisions used a median of 28 SQL queries each. With 100 active allocations, median latency was 108 ms (95th sample 428 ms); with all 1,000 active, median was 115 ms (95th sample 132 ms). These are local machine measurements, not production service levels. An adversarial case with 999 lifetime-capped allocations and one eligible allocation initially took 48.2 seconds and 4,223 queries. Database-side lifetime-cap prefiltering reduced that measured case to 599 ms and 29 queries while retaining locked checks for concurrent reservations. Daily cap, session cap, campaign targeting, and promotion checks can still make sparse eligible inventory expensive because the selector inspects candidates one by one. Monitor decision latency and query counts before a larger rollout; benchmark under production-like data distribution and concurrency in staging.
